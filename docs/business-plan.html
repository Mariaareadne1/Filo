Filo · Business Plan · v1.0

The thread, made profitable.

Students build the map for free. The businesses on it have never been able to see the word-of-mouth that feeds them. That gap is the business.

Directional — every financial figure below is a labeled assumption, not an audited projection. Part 1 is strategy & model; Part 2 is the buildable Phase-2 spec.

Part 1 — Strategy

01  The thread is two-sided

Filo's consumer app turns a student's social life into a map — every pin is a place a real, named friend actually went. Students build that map for free because it's useful to them. The business opportunity is the exhaust of that behavior.

  • Demand (students) already do the work — pinning, ranking, sharing. This is the moat: a live, trusted local graph no ad network can fake.
  • Supply (venues) is the money — businesses being pinned have never been able to see, measure, or act on the word-of-mouth driving most of their new customers.

The flywheel: more students → more pins → richer proof of who's discovering each venue → more venues claim & pay for insight → venue-backed student offers → more reasons to open Filo → more students. Neither side pays to bootstrap the other — the data already exists.

02  The business problem

For a Morningside barbershop, café, or bar, word-of-mouth is the #1 growth engine — and it's completely invisible. Yelp and Google give anonymous reviews and a star average. Instagram gives followers, not customers. Nobody can tell that barber: "34 Columbia students pinned you this semester, and 9 found you through one student's board."

Filo already holds exactly that data. The business product isn't a new data-collection chore for the venue — it's a read layer over what students already generate. That's the whole wedge.

03  Positioning & market

Filo is for social-discovery venues — found through friends, visited on a recommendation, talked about unprompted. Not necessity businesses.

Beachhead — Morningside Heights. Assumption ~150–250 qualifying venues sit within a mile of campus. Small enough to sell door-to-door, dense enough that word travels between owners. Expansion is campus-by-campus (NYU, Fordham, then other metros) — the consumer app's invite-only campus model is the business go-to-market segmentation.

04  Why Filo wins

  • The graph is the moat. Anyone can build a dashboard; almost no one can build the trusted student discovery graph beneath it. Filo builds the hard thing first, monetizes the easy thing second.
  • Zero marginal data cost. The insight a venue pays for is a byproduct of student behavior — no sensors, no data entry.
  • Attribution nobody else has. Pins attach to named users, boards, and a follow graph, so Filo can answer "where did this customer come from?" at the person/board level — which Yelp and Google structurally cannot.

Break-even is small. Illustrative at ~$60 ARPA and ~$400/mo burn, ~7 paying venues covers infra; ~35–40 supports a founder salary. A 200-venue beachhead at 30% claimed → 30% paid ≈ ~$1,000–1,300 MRR per campus — thin alone, but it's the proof unit that makes campus #2..N repeatable.

The 200-user / 500-pin consumer milestone is the sales asset. Below that density the pitch is a promise; above it, you print a real venue's real discovery data and lead with proof. The consumer KPI and the sales-readiness KPI are the same number.

07  Go-to-market

The cold open is the product. The founder walks into a Morningside venue with a one-page printout of that venue's own Filo discovery data. No other analytics tool can cold-open with the prospect's own customer data.

  • Claim-your-venue self-serve — the venue already exists as a places row; owner verifies and sees the Free dashboard instantly.
  • First 10 venues hand-sold, in person, walking the blocks around campus — these become references.
  • Peer proof travels a dense block — owners on one street talk; visible "claimed on Filo" + student offers create local FOMO.
  • Expand to paid once the owner asks who and how — the Pro trigger.

Risks & mitigations

  • Chicken-and-egg. Never sell the business side before consumer density exists — the 200/500 gate is a prerequisite, not a target.
  • Data thinness. Lead sales with the densest venues; let the long tail claim free and grow into paid.
  • Privacy/trust. The dashboard is aggregate + public-only by construction, enforced in the database (Part 2 §6) — a feature to market to students.
  • Owner isn't analytics-native. Free is one number, not a chart wall; the weekly AI action removes interpretation.

1 · Venue claiming & verification

A business owner signs up (separate auth role), searches for their venue — which usually already exists as a places row (created when the first student pinned it) — and claims it. Verification, cheapest-first: email-domain match (auto), then phone/Google-Business, then manual founder approval while volume is low.

2 · Data-model deltas

The consumer schema is untouched — a thin business layer sits on top.

create table businesses (
  id            uuid primary key default gen_random_uuid(),
  owner_auth_id uuid not null,               -- business-role auth user
  place_id      uuid references places(id),  -- the claimed venue
  name          text not null,
  tier          text not null default 'free'
                 check (tier in ('free','pro','premium')),
  unique(place_id)                            -- one owner per venue
);
create table venue_claims (
  id uuid primary key default gen_random_uuid(),
  business_id uuid references businesses(id) on delete cascade,
  place_id uuid references places(id),
  method text,                               -- email_domain | phone | manual
  status text default 'pending'
         check (status in ('pending','verified','rejected'))
);
-- Premium (defined now, built later): offers + promoted_pins

Analytics access path: the dashboard never queries raw consumer tables. It calls SECURITY DEFINER RPCs that (a) verify the caller owns a verified claim on the place_id, and (b) return only aggregate/already-public data.

3 · Discovery-analytics logic

Who discovered you this week — count + delta

this_week  = count(distinct pins.user_id)
             where place_id = $venue and created_at >= now() - 7d
delta      = this_week - prior_7d_week

One number + an ↑/↓ delta. Aggregate only — "34 students," never the roster.

Where discovery came from — attribution

Walk two public signals: which public boards contain the venue's pin (ranked by reach), and how many of this week's pinners follow an earlier public pinner of the same venue (the word-of-mouth chain). Private boards and private pins are never traversed.

Category rank near campus

rank $venue among places where category = $venue.category
     and place within the Columbia bbox
     ordered by count(distinct pinner) desc   -- "#2 cafe near campus"

5 · Build steps

  • Step 1 — Schema & RLS. Add the business tables; a business row is readable/writable only by its owner_auth_id. Test: two businesses can't read each other.
  • Step 2 — Claim & verify. Search places, create a venue_claims row, auto-verify on email-domain match. Test: matching domain flips claim to verified.
  • Step 3 — Discovery RPCs. Three SECURITY DEFINER RPCs (counts, sources, rank), each asserting a verified claim, returning aggregate/public only. Test: unverified caller → forbidden; private pins never appear.
  • Step 4 — 'This Week' dashboard. Consume the RPCs in the Filo sticker/serif system; locked Pro cards below. Test: dense venue shows non-zero count; unclaimed venue can't load it.
  • Step 5 — Billing. Stripe subscriptions → businesses.tier; gate Pro/Premium RPCs on tier. Test: downgrade re-locks Pro.

Enforced at the database layer: dashboards read only through SECURITY DEFINER RPCs that check a verified claim and emit counts/public rows only. "Businesses see the crowd, never the person" — a trust promise Filo markets to students.

Phase-3 preview — the AI action agent

Once Discovery Analytics, Superfans + Drift, and Offers exist, the data supports one AI-suggested action per week per venue — "post your lavender latte, it's your most-pinned item among first-years" or "your Tuesdays are cold; run a student happy-hour." It turns a dashboard a busy owner forgets to open into a weekly nudge that does the interpreting — the bridge from analytics tool to growth partner.

Filo — build the consumer side, get the pins, then show businesses the thread.
